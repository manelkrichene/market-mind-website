Brand strategy

Positioning is a choice, not a list of adjectives.

A practical brand positioning framework for choosing an audience, a relevant problem, a differentiated promise and proof that makes it credible.

MARKET MIND FIELD NOTES7 MIN READOCTOBER 2026

“Innovative, premium, trusted, customer-centric” can describe almost any brand. Adjectives may set a tone, but rarely explain why a particular customer should choose one business instead of another.

Positioning is more useful when treated as a set of strategic choices: who the business is most relevant to, which problem it addresses, what difference it offers and why that difference is credible.

Choose the audience with a reason

A brand cannot be equally relevant to every person in every situation. Define the audience in terms of meaningful needs, context, behaviour or constraints, not only broad demographics. Ask which group experiences the problem strongly, recognizes the benefit and can realistically access the offer.

Identify the tension that makes the offer matter

People choose products and services to make progress, reduce friction, avoid risk, express identity or reach an outcome. Look at the alternatives buyers use and the compromises they accept. A sharper positioning often emerges from a specific tension inside a broad need.

Differentiate on a dimension buyers care about

Difference is not automatically valuable. A brand can be unique in a way customers do not notice or pay for. A strong choice connects a meaningful buyer priority with a difference the business can consistently deliver.

Make the promise believable

A value proposition becomes stronger when supported by proof: process, expertise, demonstrations, product design, transparent constraints or customer evidence shared with permission. Claims should never outrun the evidence behind them.

Turn the strategy into a usable statement

  1. For: the priority audience and relevant situation.
  2. Who need: the problem, job or tension to address.
  3. We provide: the distinctive value and outcome.
  4. Because: the proof or capability that makes the promise credible.

This is an internal tool, not necessarily final homepage copy. It should help teams decide which claims to emphasize, audiences to prioritize, product features to improve and language to test.

Positioning should guide trade-offs

If a positioning statement does not change a decision, it may be too generic. A meaningful position influences product priorities, pricing logic, channels, customer experience and communication. It also makes some tempting opportunities less relevant.

That is why positioning is a choice: it concentrates attention around a promise the business can prove and a customer can understand.
