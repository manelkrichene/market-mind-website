Making B2B growth more repeatable

An anonymized commercial strategy engagement focused on structuring a specialist producer's offer, target segments and prospecting process beyond founder-led relationships.

The challenge

A specialist producer had established early professional demand, but commercial activity remained heavily dependent on the founder's personal network. The next challenge was not simply to increase promotional activity. It was to make the commercial process easier to repeat, prioritize and manage while respecting production and operational constraints.

Start with the commercial reality

Before expanding the prospect list, the work needs a grounded view of the offer and formats, sales patterns, margins and costs where data is available, operational capacity, delivery constraints, buying frequency and characteristics of existing accounts. Where information is missing, the gap should become a data-collection task rather than an invented assumption.

Structure the offer around buying situations

Different professional buyers may need different ways to buy. A high-volume buyer, a recurring food-service buyer and a specialist account can differ in order size, frequency, specifications, service expectations and decision process. Offer architecture should make those differences visible without unnecessary complexity.

A practical structure can include formats, volume logic, reorder expectations, product specifications, delivery terms and rules for requests outside the standard offer. Pricing recommendations should use actual costs and margin requirements, not guesses from competitor prices alone.

Turn prospecting into a managed process

  • Target matrix: segment accounts by fit, buying potential, geography and operational suitability.
  • Prospecting sheet: keep account data, contact history, status and next action together.
  • Message logic: communicate the most relevant value proposition for each buying situation.
  • Review cadence: track activity and movement through the pipeline, not only the number of messages sent.

Measure progress without overclaiming

Early indicators can include qualified accounts added, conversations started, samples or quotations requested, trial orders, repeat orders and the reasons opportunities stall. Revenue and margin matter, but leading indicators help reveal which part of the system needs attention.

Because the engagement is confidential and performance figures have not been approved for publication, this case focuses on the strategic problem and approach rather than claiming growth results.

The strategic lesson

Commercial growth becomes more manageable when the offer, target customer, process and measurement system are designed together. More outreach can create activity; a coherent commercial system makes that activity easier to learn from.

Publication note. The client is anonymized. No revenue, margin, production or conversion outcomes are published or implied.
